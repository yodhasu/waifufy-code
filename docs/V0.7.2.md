# Waifufy Code 0.7.2: she talks first

In 0.7 casual mode only ever answers. 0.7.2 lets her start: she decides on her own when to say something, what to say, and whether to say nothing, like someone you're texting. She holds off while you type, notices when you've gone away, and reacts to that the way her personality would. On the phone her messages arrive as notifications you can answer from the notification itself.

Everything else stays as in 0.7.1: one session, one history and one memory. Her own messages are casual lines like any other.

## Her own turns (`casual/initiative.ts`)

Casual mode only. Never in work mode, never while work runs, and never while she is already answering.

- **When.** After every exchange, and after each message she sends on her own, she picks the next moment she might speak. It comes from her traits and state, not a fixed timer:
  - sooner the more `clingy` she is, the closer she feels (affection), and the more she misses you (sadness, loneliness);
  - later when she's annoyed, and for a high `mask` (a tsundere doesn't want to look eager);
  - with random spread, so it never feels scheduled.
  As a rough guide, a very clingy preset might speak again after 2–8 minutes and a calm one after 30–90.
- **Her turn.** When the moment comes and you aren't typing, she gets a turn with no message from you. The per-turn note says how long since you last wrote, whether you seem to be here or away, and how many messages she has sent since you last answered. She then decides, as herself, to:
  - say something: a thought, a question, something she remembered, a reaction to the time of day;
  - send two or three short messages in a row, delivered a moment apart like real texting;
  - look something up first with her existing tools (the read-only toolkit with your Slack, calendar and the web; `remember`, `recall`, `search_history`), then tell you about it if it's worth it;
  - stay quiet, with `<quiet/>` after the appraisal line. Nothing is shown, and the turn costs a few tokens.
- **Same machinery.** The hidden `<feel>` appraisal, faces, voice, affection tags and the journal work as on any casual turn.

## Typing and away

- **Typing holds her.** Both composers (desktop and phone) send `typing` on and off to the link, throttled. While any client is typing, her turn waits. When typing stops without a message, her turn runs with a fresh note. If you sent something, your message comes first and her moment is picked again after her reply.
- **Away.** You count as away when you've been typing for 20 minutes without sending, or after a stretch with no message and no typing. How long that stretch is follows her personality: a clingy one notices sooner.
- **While you're away, her personality decides.** A very clingy character keeps reaching out, stretching the gaps slowly. A tsundere sends one huffy line and then waits. A calm one waits until you're back and says so. A pushy one might ping you a lot. There's no built-in cap: that's the personality's call.
- **Your boundaries win.** A new **Boundaries** setting, in your own words (for example "nothing after 23:00", "at most two messages while I'm at work"), goes into her prompt as rules she never breaks. A **She can message first** switch, on by default, turns the whole thing off.
- **Safety ceiling.** A hidden limit of 30 of her own turns an hour stops a runaway loop if something breaks. It's not a personality cap: real behavior should never get near it.

## Phone: notifications and reply

- **Notifications.** Native Android code (`PhoneLink.kt` next to `MainActivity.kt`), not a plugin, so they look like a chat app's: one conversation thread (`MessagingStyle`) with her name and the text of each casual line (tags removed), while the phone app isn't in front. The page drives it through `window.WaifufyAndroid` (`app/src/phone-notify.ts`); on the desktop there is no bridge and nothing happens. The app asks for Android's notification permission when the phone joins a session, and opening the app clears the thread.
- **Reply from the notification.** The thread has an inline **Reply** (`RemoteInput`, `ReplyReceiver.kt`). What you type is added to the thread and handed to the page (`window.__waifufyReply`), which sends it as a normal message, so her answer is a normal casual turn with the same history, memory and feelings. If the app was swiped away, the thread says to open it instead.
- **Staying connected.** Android freezes background apps within minutes, and then nothing reaches the phone. While the phone is joined to a session, a foreground service keeps the connection to the desktop open. It shows one quiet "Connected to {her name}" notification, the way companion and chat apps do. `KeepAliveService.kt`, type `remoteMessaging`, started when the phone joins and stopped when it leaves or the app is swiped away. The WebView keeps running JavaScript in the background (Wry pauses the view, not its timers), so the page's connection does the rest. Push through Google's servers (Firebase) is not used: it would need a Google project and a relay outside your machines.

## Memory and learning

- Her own messages go into the transcript as casual blocks with only her side, and into the journal marked `initiative: true`.
- Learning reads them like any other exchange. The reflection also judges reaching out: messages you answered warmly become "works" notes, ignored or brushed-off ones become "avoid" notes. So over time she learns your rhythm within her personality.

## Requirements

- **R83:** in casual mode, with "She can message first" on, she sends messages on her own at moments picked from her traits and state, and can also choose to stay quiet, which shows nothing.
- **R84:** while any client is typing, her own turn waits, and it runs once typing stops without a message.
- **R85:** after 20 minutes of typing without sending, or a personality-dependent stretch with no activity, she treats you as away and acts on it as her personality would, within your Boundaries.
- **R86:** she never sends her own messages in work mode, while work runs, or with the switch off.
- **R87:** on the phone, her messages arrive as notifications while the app isn't in front, and an inline Reply sends a casual message.
- **R88:** while joined to a session, the phone stays connected in the background, with one ongoing notification.

## Tests

- `tests/unit/initiative.test.mjs`: on a fake clock, picking the moment from traits (clingy sooner, proud or annoyed later, unanswered messages stretching a calm heart's gaps), holding while typing, away after 20 minutes of typing or a long silence (R85), never when not allowed, and the safety ceiling. Then the whole loop against a real link in mock mode with her clock sped up (`WC_INITIATIVE_SCALE`): never in work mode, two messages of hers in casual mode, journaled as hers, held by a draft, and stopped by going back to work.
- `tests/e2e/scenarios/28-initiative.mjs`: R83, R84 and R86 in the app with the mock provider.
- Her own turns run in mock mode only when `WC_INITIATIVE_SCALE` is set, so other tests never see them.
- Phone (R87, R88): checked by hand on a device. Notification actions and the foreground service can't run in the desktop test suite.

## Open questions

- R87 and R88 need a device check: the APK builds and its manifest has the service, receiver and permissions, but notifications, Reply and staying connected haven't run on a phone yet.
- Android 14 requires a declared foreground-service type, and Google Play reviews those types. That doesn't matter for a sideloaded APK.
- Battery: a foreground service with an open connection costs a little. It runs only while the phone is joined to a session.
