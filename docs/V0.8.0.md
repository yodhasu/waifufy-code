# Waifufy Code 0.8.0: waifu cards, and her in 3D

Up to 0.7.2 the app had one character, spread across Settings. 0.8.0 bundles everything that makes her *her* into a waifu card, so you can keep several characters and swap between them, and a card can draw her as a 3D MMD model instead of a Live2D costume. Messages you send while she works now reach her the way they do in the CLI.

## Waifu cards (`link/src/cards.ts`, Settings → Cards)

- **A card is one whole character:** her Waifu-tab persona, her Roleplay presets, her model (a Live2D costume, or a 3D `.pmx`), her voice (voice id, language, speed, Japanese style notes) and her **love meter**.
- **Swap between sessions.** Settings → Cards lists them, with the love meter, the model kind and whether she has a voice. Switch, New card (the default character, an empty meter), Duplicate current, Delete (not the one in use). A swap is refused while a session runs: the running session keeps the persona and meter it started with. Resuming a save swaps to the card it was played with.
- **The love meter moved from each save to the card.** It carries from one New Game to the next, and each card has its own. Saves keep a copy for the Load page.
- **Your personal save stays out of the card.** What she remembers about you (casual memory) and her mood live in the data folder under the card's id, never in a card file. Your profile and the app's settings stay the same whichever card is in.
- **Export and import.** Export writes a `.waifu.json`. Paths on your computer don't travel: the models folder is left out, and a 3D model keeps only its file name, which an import finds again next to the card file. Import always makes a new card, so it never overwrites one you have.
- **Upgrading:** the first launch makes a card from your current settings. It keeps the id her memory already lives under (her name), so she keeps what she knows, and its love meter starts from your latest save.
- `settings.json` stays the live copy of the active card: every settings change is written back to it, and a swap copies the new card's fields in. A window that hasn't heard of a swap yet can't write the old character into the new card.

## Her in 3D (`app/src/character/mmd.ts`, `mmd-engine.ts`)

- **A card can draw her as an MMD model.** Settings → Appearance → Model: Live2D, or 3D (MMD) with the path to her `.pmx`. babylon-mmd draws it with MMD shading, outlines, self-shadows and Bullet physics (hair, skirt, accessories), full height in the character box, on the title, chat and Settings screens.
- **She acts as on Live2D.** The 15 emotions are her face morphs plus small head, neck, shoulder and chest stances, with a beat for some (a nod, a bounce, the pout's turn away). She breathes, sways and blinks, her head and eyes follow the cursor, her mouth follows the voice (or flaps with the text), app events show a short emotion, a headpat makes her shy and a tap surprises her.
- **Live2D-only parts are hidden on a 3D card** (costumes, the models folder). If the model can't load, her Live2D costume stands in.
- The 3D code (~1.6 MB) loads only for a 3D card. The link serves the folder of the active card's `.pmx`, model and texture files only.
- Models aren't bundled: bring your own and respect their license.

Known limits: headpats don't escalate yet and show no hearts, there is no working pose while she's busy, the expressions use the morph names of Reine's model (another model may show fewer), and the framing assumes her height. The physics world stays loaded after you switch a card back to Live2D (disposing it hangs babylon-mmd); it is about 6 MB and reused by the next 3D card.

## Messages while she works, as in the CLI

- **Enter** reaches her at her next step: it goes to her at once and she reads it when the tool call running now finishes. A dashed ➤ chip shows it until then.
- **Ctrl+X Enter** holds a message until her turn ends (the old Enter). × removes one; **↑** takes them back into the input.
- **Send now** (Ctrl+Enter, Ctrl+X Ctrl+S, or the button on the queue): what you held and the draft, as one message, at once. Work that can go to the background goes there; otherwise her turn stops for it.
- **Esc** interrupts, and what you held goes out next, instead of coming back to the input.
- **Ctrl+S** stashes the draft; on an empty prompt it brings it back.
- The queue sits centered on the input's top edge, on one line, so it no longer runs into the quick menu.
